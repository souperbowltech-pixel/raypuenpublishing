## Why each line is there

**The household and classroom exception.** This is the one clause I would not leave out. Without it,
a teacher who photocopies a colouring page for a second child is technically in breach, and a parent
who prints a second copy of the sticker sheet is too. You are selling a book that is meant to be
copied within a family and a classroom, so the licence should say so. It also quietly makes the book
easier for a school to buy, because a head teacher reading a flat "no reproduction" clause has to
think twice.

**The ISBN.** Every retailer and library needs one, and IngramSpark will not take a book without it.
You already hold a Bowker account from Book 1. Book 2 needs **its own** ISBN. The same number cannot
be reused, and a different format of the same book, such as a hardback, needs another one again.

**"First Edition, 2026."** When the six new pages or a reprint change anything, that line becomes
"Second Edition" and the year changes. It is how a librarian tells two printings apart.

**"Printed in the United States of America."** Required for import and customs purposes once the
book crosses a border, which it will if any copies go to Nepal.

**The granddaughters' credit** uses your exact wording and their middle names only, as their mother
permitted. It must stay word for word.

## The one decision only you can make

**Who holds the copyright?** Three candidates and they are not interchangeable:

- **Ray Puen** personally. Simplest, and usual for an author who has not assigned rights to a company.
- **Regency Press.** Correct if the rights sit with the publishing business.
- **New Life Mission Board, Inc.** Correct if the ministry commissioned the work and owns it.

This decides who can license the book, who sues if it is copied, and what happens to the rights
later. It is a question for your lawyer rather than for me, and it is worth asking him while he is
already looking at the Sunergos matter.

## Two things to fix elsewhere, while this is being set

**The Parent's Guide quotes scripture and may need a permission line.** The earlier draft used the
King James Version ("Open thou mine eyes, that I may behold wondrous things out of thy law"), which
is public domain in the United States and needs nothing. The newer draft reads "Open my eyes, that I
may see wondrous things from Your law", which is New King James. **NKJV is copyrighted and requires a
permission notice on the copyright page.** Decide which translation the series uses and keep to it,
because mixing them means carrying every publisher's notice.

**Book 2's interior carries no scripture**, only story couplets, so this applies to the Guide rather
than to Book 2 itself.
